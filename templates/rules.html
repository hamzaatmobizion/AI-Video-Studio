Prompt rules

Follow the rules for the screen you use, word for word. The easiest way: copy its example, then change only the story, the people and the lines.

Google Flow screen (8-second clips) · Gemini Studio screen (10-second clips)

Google Flow screen

Every clip is 8 seconds. A prompt written in time steps is kept exactly as you wrote it, so write it this way.

1. Write the prompt in this order

  1. First line: length, shape, kind of video and style. The length is 8, 16, 24… seconds.
    “A 16-second vertical 9:16 Pakistani Punjabi family comedy, Pixar-style 3D cartoon animation.”
  2. Setting: one place, the time of day and at most 3 objects, saying where each one is.
  3. People and voices (see 3).
  4. Time steps of 8 seconds: “0–8 seconds:”, “8–16 seconds:”… in order, one for each clip, each starting with the camera.
  5. Last line: starts with “Sound:” (see 7).

2. Lines

  • Everyday Pakistani Punjabi written in Shahmukhi, the way people really talk, not Urdu translated word for word.
  • The speaker's name before every line: Name says: "line"
  • At most about 14 spoken words per step with two people talking, 16 with one, never more than 20.
  • Short, natural and full of feeling. No long speeches, and never add lines just to fill the time.
  • Nobody talks while eating, chewing or laughing: they finish first, and laughter comes after the last line.

3. People

  • At most 2 people in a clip (3 at most). Tick each of them in Cast.
  • Picked in Cast (with a portrait): write only their voice: “Bahu — Voice: a woman in her late 20s, cheeky, Pakistani Punjabi accent.” Never describe their face or clothes: the portrait is used as it is.
  • Not in your library: age, hair, face, clothes with colours, then a Voice line.
  • Always give an adult age (“in her late 20s”, “in his 50s”), never just “young”.
  • Same face, age, hair, clothes, body and voice in every step. Never change them partway.
  • Say who is on the left and who is on the right in the first step, and never swap them.
  • Give people who talk clearly different voices (“high and light”, “low and husky”). Two voices alike get mixed up, and one person ends up saying the other's line.

4. Camera, place and flow from clip to clip

  • Start each step with the camera: shot, angle and move. “Camera: medium shot, eye level, static.” Use static or slow push-in; a tracking shot only for running. No sudden jumps.
  • Keep the main people and the main action clearly in the frame.
  • The place, light, background and objects stay the same. An object stays where the last step left it, and nothing disappears.
  • Each step carries on from where the last one ended. Don't reset the scene, change the place or walk people back in.

5. Action and acting

  • Say exactly what each person does, where they stand or sit, and which hand holds what. One or two actions per step; don't crowd it.
  • In a vehicle or a room, say exactly where each person is (“driving”, “standing on the bus door steps”, “in the back seat”), not just left or right, and where the things they use are (“the only steering wheel, in front of the driver”). Otherwise Flow may give two people a steering wheel each.
  • Give each step a clear face, gesture or reaction that matches the line (“eyes wide”, “hand on her chest”, “grins”).
  • The action fits the lines and the time step it's in. People handle objects and each other naturally.
  • Nobody claps, points or waves while holding something: they put it down first.
  • At most one full turn or spin per step: when someone is seen from behind for long, their hair and face change.

6. Style and genre

  • Pick the style, camera, light, mood and sound that fit your story: family comedy, emotional drama, romance, village life, horror and suspense, thriller and mystery, action and adventure, a moral or educational story, children's animation, or a social message.
  • Comedy and Pixar style are only the example. Use them only when you want them.

7. Sound

  • The last line starts with “Sound:” and has sound effects only: the place, footsteps, objects, reactions, cartoon effects.
  • No music in the prompt. For music, add a Background music file.
  • A dance or song video too: each clip would make its own music, so the beat would jump at every join. Upload the track as Background music, write “dances to a steady dhol beat” in the steps, and keep Sound: to footsteps, ghungroo and claps.

8. Don't write

  • A colour palette when you use portraits.
  • “Realistic” for a cartoon.
  • Hitting or slapping.
  • On-screen text, subtitles, captions, logos, or writing on signs, vehicles or walls (it comes out garbled).
  • Lists of rules inside the prompt.
  • Extra people or actions the story doesn't need.

9. Before you press Generate

  • Every character in the story is ticked in Cast.
  • “Make the clips myself” is ticked.
  • Read the whole prompt: the actions and lines match their time steps, and the last line starts with “Sound:”.
  • Fix every red or yellow note under the prompt.
  • Add a Background music file if you want music.

Examples (Bahu and Ammi ticked in Cast)

An 8-second video is one time step. A longer video is the same thing with more steps: 16 seconds has two, 24 seconds three, and so on, each carrying on from the one before.

8 seconds (one clip)
An 8-second vertical 9:16 Pakistani Punjabi family comedy, Pixar-style 3D cartoon animation.
Setting: a cosy village kitchen in the morning, a small wooden table in the middle, a steel cup of tea on the table.

Bahu — Voice: a woman in her late 20s, cheeky and sweet, Pakistani Punjabi accent.
Ammi — Voice: a woman in her 60s, loud and dramatic, Pakistani Punjabi accent.

0–8 seconds: Camera: medium two-shot, eye level, slow push-in, ending close on both faces. Ammi sits on a stool on the right of the table, picks up the steel cup with her right hand and takes a sip. Bahu stands on the left with her hands folded, smiling hopefully. Ammi's eyes go wide and her face puckers, and she puts the cup down on the table. Ammi says: "نی! چاہ وچ کھنڈ پائی اے کہ لون؟" Bahu gasps and bites her lip. Bahu says: "ہائے ربا، لون!"

Sound: a long slurp, a cartoon gulp, the cup clinking on the table, a short gasp.
24 seconds (three clips)
A 24-second vertical 9:16 Pakistani Punjabi family comedy, Pixar-style 3D cartoon animation.
Setting: a cosy village courtyard in the afternoon, a woven charpai in the middle, a broom leaning on the wall behind.

Bahu — Voice: a woman in her late 20s, cheeky and sweet, Pakistani Punjabi accent.
Ammi — Voice: a woman in her 60s, loud and emotional, Pakistani Punjabi accent.

0–8 seconds: Camera: medium shot, eye level, static. Ammi sits on the right side of the charpai, holding a mobile phone in both hands, staring at it with teary eyes and a trembling lip. Bahu walks in on the left, empty-handed, stops and puts her hands on her hips. Bahu says: "امی جی! میرا فون؟" Ammi, not looking up, sniffs and says: "چپ کر، ہیرو مرن لگا اے!"

8–16 seconds: Camera: medium two-shot, slow push-in, ending close on both faces. Bahu sits beside Ammi on the left side of the charpai and leans in to look at the screen. Bahu says: "امی جی، ایہہ ڈرامہ نئیں، اشتہار اے!" Ammi freezes, holds the phone in her left hand and wipes her tears with her right hand. Ammi says: "ہائے! فیر وی بڑا دکھی سی!" Bahu rolls her eyes and shakes her head with a big grin.

16–24 seconds: Camera: close two-shot, eye level, static. Ammi hands the phone to Bahu with her left hand, folds her arms and turns her face away in a sulk. Bahu takes it in her right hand, taps the screen with her left finger and holds it up between them. Bahu says: "لو امی جی، اصلی ڈرامہ لا دتا!" Ammi slowly turns back, her eyes shining, and leans in close. Ammi says: "چل ہن چپ کر کے ویکھ!" They sit shoulder to shoulder, watching the phone.

Sound: dramatic sobbing from the phone, a loud sniff, a soft tap on the screen, a cartoon boing at the end.

Gemini Studio screen

Every clip is 10 seconds. Gemini turns your prompt into a script of 10-second scenes.

  1. First line: length, shape, kind of video and one style. The length is 10, 20, 30… seconds.
    “A 20-second vertical 9:16 Pakistani Punjabi family comedy, Pixar-style 3D cartoon animation.”
  2. Setting: one place, the time of day and at most 3 objects, saying where each one is.
  3. People: at most 3 in a clip.
    • Picked from your library (with a portrait): write only their voice; never describe their face or clothes.
    • Not in your library: age, hair, face, clothes with colours, then a Voice line, as in the example.
    • Always give an adult age (“in his 20s”, “in his 50s”), never just “young”.
  4. Time steps of 10 seconds: “0–10 seconds:”, “10–20 seconds:”… one step for each clip.
  5. Each step starts with the camera: “Camera: medium shot, eye level, static.”
  6. Left and right: say who is where in the first step, and never swap them.
  7. One or two actions per step. Say which hand holds what; put things down before clapping, pointing or waving.
  8. Lines: Name says: "line", in Pakistani Punjabi written in Shahmukhi. At most about 25 spoken words per step.
  9. Last line, Sound: sound effects only. No music in the prompt: add a Background music file instead.
  10. Don't write: “realistic” in a cartoon, hitting or slapping, lists of rules, or text and signs on screen.
  11. Before you press Generate: tick your characters in Cast and fix any red or yellow note under the prompt.
Full example for Gemini Studio (20 seconds, no characters picked)
A 20-second vertical 9:16 Pakistani Punjabi family comedy, Pixar-style 3D cartoon animation.
Setting: a small village shop in the morning, a wooden counter, a glass jar of toffees on the counter.

Chacha: a man in his 50s, round belly, grey mustache, white shalwar kameez and a brown waistcoat.
Voice: deep and slow, Pakistani Punjabi accent.
Pappu: a man in his 20s, thin, spiky black hair, light-blue shalwar kameez.
Voice: quick and cheeky, Pakistani Punjabi accent.

0–10 seconds: Camera: medium shot, eye level, static. Chacha stands behind the counter on the right, dozing with his chin on his left hand. Pappu walks in on the left and taps the counter with his right hand. Pappu says: "چاچا جی، اک ٹافی دینا، ادھار!" Chacha opens one eye. Chacha says: "پچھلا ادھار کدوں دئیں گا؟"

10–20 seconds: Camera: medium two-shot, slow push-in, ending close on both faces. Pappu grins and puts a coin on the counter with his right hand. Pappu says: "اج ای! اے لو پورا اک روپیہ!" Chacha picks up the coin with his left hand, looks at it closely and bursts out laughing. Chacha says: "پتر، اے تے انیس سو نوے دا سکہ اے!"

Sound: a shop bell as Pappu walks in, a coin clink on the counter, a cartoon boing at the end.
