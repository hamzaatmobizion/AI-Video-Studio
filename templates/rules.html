How to write a prompt for the best video

Videos are made in short clips that are joined together: 10-second clips in the Studio (Gemini), 8-second clips on the Flow screen (Google Flow). Each clip is made on its own. The app already does most of the work of keeping them consistent; a few things only you can do.

The app does this for you

No need to write these in your prompt.

  • Splits your story into clips (10 seconds with Gemini, 8 with Google Flow), keeping every timed moment and every line you wrote.
  • Keeps faces, clothes, places and objects the same from clip to clip: your characters' portraits, a story bible, and each clip starting from the previous one's last frame.
  • Gives each picked character the part that fits them, and tells you on the script page if one had no part.
  • Tells Gemini the rules it often breaks: one action at a time, two hands each, things keep their state, no on-screen text, no crowds or fast fights in close-up.
  • Ends every clip prompt with the master rules below in short form: a smooth, steady camera, real gravity, constant light, no jumps or slow motion, and nothing added that the scene doesn't ask for. A clip that starts from the previous clip's last frame is told to begin its action naturally from it. A cartoon gets the 3D cartoon rules instead: cartoon physics, models and textures that never deform, and no switch to photorealism or another style.
  • Makes a “young man” or “young woman” look clearly adult.
  • When it writes the script, rewrites your prompt into scenes by these rules: lines too long for one clip move to the next scene, invented people get clothes with colours and a voice, “realistic” is kept out of a cartoon, Pakistani Punjabi lines are written in Shahmukhi, and each scene ends on a clear reaction.
  • Uses the voice your prompt gives a character, else their saved one.
  • Leaves music out of the clips (it would change at every cut) and adds your Background music track under the whole video.
  • Reads the length, style and 9:16 or 16:9 shape from your prompt.

So you can leave out long “STRICT RULES” lists: they make the prompt longer, not the video better.

What only you can do

RuleExample
At most 2 moments per clip (10 seconds with Gemini, 8 with Flow). A moment is one action and its reaction or line. Give running, chasing, jumping or fighting its own clip. 10 s → 2 moments, 20 s → 4, 30 s → 6
Keep lines short: about 25 spoken words per 10-second Gemini clip, everyone together. In an 8-second Flow clip, 16 when one person speaks and 14 when two or more do. In Chinese, Japanese or Thai, every 2 characters count as a word. Split a long speech across two clips.
Use characters with good portraits: full body, in the same style as the video. Name them in the prompt as they're named in your library. A Pixar-style 3D portrait for a cartoon video.
Adults must look adult. If Gemini takes someone for a child, it refuses any scene with romance, shouting, chasing or hitting. “a man in his 30s with a thick mustache”, not “a boy” or “a young lad”
Describe clothes with colours for anyone without a portrait. “red and teal striped sari”, not “sari”
One style, and no “realistic” in a cartoon. It makes Gemini treat your characters as real people and refuse. “Pixar-style 3D cartoon animation”
Write lines in the script of the language you want spoken. For Pakistani Punjabi use Shahmukhi; Gurmukhi gives an Indian pronunciation. “نی نوہے!” not “ਨੀ ਨੂੰਹੇ!”
Say which hand does what when someone holds something and also points or waves. “holds the roti in her left hand and points with her right”
No photos of real people. Gemini refuses to animate them. Use portraits drawn on the Characters page.

Before you generate

  • Read the notes under the prompt: red means Gemini will likely refuse or fail, yellow means the video may suffer, green means nothing is known to cause problems.
  • For an important video, tick Let me review the script; for a final version, also Lock characters and Check each clip.

Ask an AI to write the prompt
You write prompts for an AI video tool that makes videos in 10-second clips and joins them into one video. Turn my idea (at the end) into one prompt in exactly the format below, following these rules:

- Length: 10, 20, 30… seconds (up to 2 minutes). At most 2 moments per 10 seconds; a moment is one action and its reaction or line. Give any fast movement (running, chasing, jumping, fighting, falling) its own 10 seconds, never with a long line.
- Dialogue: about 20–25 spoken words per 10 seconds at most, all characters together. Put each line in double quotes after who says it. For Pakistani Punjabi write the lines in Shahmukhi (Perso-Arabic) script, never Gurmukhi; for Urdu, in Urdu script.
- Characters: give each one an age, hair, face, build and clothes with colours, and how they sound. Adults must clearly look adult: an age like "in his 30s" and a grown-up feature (mustache, stubble, a mature face). No children in scenes with romance, shouting, chasing or hitting. No real or famous people.
- Style: name one style, e.g. "Pixar-style 3D cartoon animation". Never use the word "realistic" for a cartoon.
- Actions: one clear action at a time. If someone holds something in both hands they can't also point or wave: say which hand does what. Objects, clothes and places keep their state until the story changes them.
- Sound: sound effects only, no background music, no subtitles, no on-screen text.
- Keep it plain and short: no lists of "strict rules", no repeated instructions.

Format:

A [length]-second [vertical 9:16 or widescreen 16:9] [kind of video, e.g. Pakistani Punjabi family comedy], [style].
Setting: [place, time of day, 2 or 3 key objects].

[Name]: [age], [hair, face, build], [clothes with colours].
Voice: [e.g. deep, warm, Pakistani Punjabi accent].
(one block like this for each character)

0–5 seconds: [one action]. [Name] says: "[line]"
5–10 seconds: [one action or reaction]. [Name] says: "[line]"
(continue in 5-second steps to the end)

Sound: [sound effects]. No background music, no subtitles.

My idea: [write your idea here]
Or fill in the format yourself

Setting: [place, time of day, 2 or 3 key objects].

[Name]: [age, e.g. a man in his 30s], [hair, face, build], [clothes with colours].
Voice: [how they sound, e.g. deep, warm, Pakistani Punjabi accent].

0–5 seconds: [one action]. [Name] says: "[line]"
5–10 seconds: [one action or reaction]. [Name] says: "[line]"
10–15 seconds: [one action]. [Name] says: "[line]"

Example

A 20-second vertical 9:16 Pakistani Punjabi family comedy, Pixar-style 3D cartoon animation.
Setting: a colourful village kitchen in the afternoon, steel pots on a shelf, a wooden stool.

Saas: a woman in her 50s, grey hair in a bun, round face, orange shalwar kameez and green dupatta.
Voice: older woman, loud and expressive, Pakistani Punjabi accent.
Bahu: a woman in her late 20s, long black braid, pink shalwar kameez.
Voice: young woman, cheeky and sweet, Pakistani Punjabi accent.

0–5 seconds: Saas walks in, sees Bahu on her phone and puts her hands on her hips. Saas says: "نی نوہے! سارا دن موبائل تے لگی رہندی ایں!"
5–10 seconds: Bahu hides the phone behind her back and smiles. Bahu says: "امی جی، تہاڈے لئی رشتہ لبھ رہی آں!"
10–15 seconds: Saas freezes, eyes wide. Saas says: "میرے لئی رشتہ؟ میں تاں ویاہی ہوئی آں!"
15–20 seconds: Bahu laughs and walks out of the kitchen; Saas shakes her head.

Sound: a cartoon boing at the end. No background music, no subtitles.

Master rules for any AI video

The app already adds these to every clip prompt in a few words. Copy the full list to paste under a prompt you send to another AI video tool, or under a prompt you make by hand in Flow.

AI video generation – master rules
AI VIDEO GENERATION – MASTER RULES

1. Character Consistency
Always maintain the exact identity of every character.
Preserve facial features, hairstyle, skin tone, clothing, body shape, and proportions.
Never randomly change a character's appearance.
Keep all characters visually consistent from beginning to end.

2. Scene Consistency
Maintain the original environment, background, and scene layout.
Do not introduce unexpected objects, people, or environmental changes.
Preserve the position and appearance of important objects.
Maintain visual continuity between frames.

3. Natural Movement
All movements must look natural, smooth, and realistic.
Follow realistic human and animal body mechanics.
Ensure proper walking, running, sitting, standing, and hand movements.
Avoid robotic, stiff, exaggerated, or unnatural animation.
Maintain realistic object interactions and physical movements.

4. Realistic Physics
Follow real-world gravity, momentum, weight, and motion.
Objects must interact naturally with their surroundings.
Maintain realistic collisions, contact, and movement.
Avoid floating objects, unnatural movements, and impossible physical interactions.

5. Camera Control
Use intentional, professional cinematic camera movements.
Keep camera movements smooth and controlled.
Avoid unnecessary camera shaking, random zooming, and sudden angle changes.
Use only camera movements appropriate to the scene.
Maintain consistent framing and composition unless a camera movement is required.

6. Facial Expressions
Maintain realistic facial anatomy and expressions.
Ensure natural eye movements and blinking.
Keep facial features consistent throughout the video.
Avoid distorted faces, unnatural smiles, and facial morphing.

7. Hands and Body Anatomy
Maintain anatomically correct hands, fingers, and limbs.
Avoid extra fingers, missing fingers, duplicated limbs, and distorted body parts.
Ensure natural hand gestures and body posture.
Maintain correct body proportions during movement.

8. Lighting and Shadows
Maintain consistent lighting direction and intensity.
Use realistic shadows, reflections, and highlights.
Preserve natural exposure and color balance.
Avoid sudden lighting changes and flickering.

9. Visual Quality
Maintain high visual fidelity and realistic textures.
Preserve details in skin, hair, clothing, and environmental surfaces.
Use natural depth, focus, and cinematic color grading.
Avoid pixelation, blur, visual artifacts, ghosting, and flickering.

10. Motion Continuity
Ensure smooth transitions between movements.
Maintain continuous motion without unnecessary interruptions.
Avoid sudden position changes, teleportation, and unwanted jumps.
Keep movement speed and direction consistent with the described action.

11. Start Frame Rules
Begin the video from the supplied Start Frame.
Preserve the original composition, subject identity, and environment.
Do not unnecessarily modify the initial appearance.
Begin the requested action naturally from the starting position.

12. End Frame Rules
If an End Frame is supplied, guide the video toward that exact visual composition.
Reach the final position through natural and continuous movement.
Preserve character identity and environmental consistency.
If no End Frame is supplied, generate a natural ending based on the described action.
Never invent an unnecessary ending or force an unnatural final pose.

13. Prompt and Action Accuracy
Follow the user's requested scene and actions precisely.
Do not add unrelated actions or unexpected events.
Do not introduce unnecessary characters or objects.
Prioritize the main action over decorative details.
Keep the generated video faithful to the original prompt.

14. Professional Cinematic Standards
Use professional cinematic composition.
Maintain realistic depth and perspective.
Use appropriate focal length and natural depth of field.
Keep camera movement, lighting, and subject motion visually coordinated.
Maintain a polished, professional film-quality appearance.

15. Negative Generation Rules
Avoid: character identity changes, face and body distortion, unnatural movements, extra fingers and limbs, object duplication, background morphing, flickering and ghosting, sudden camera movements, unwanted scene transitions, random objects or characters, inconsistent lighting, unrealistic physics, unnecessary slow motion, unexpected cuts or changes in composition.

16. Most Important Generation Principle
Every generated video must prioritize: character consistency, scene consistency, natural and realistic movement, accurate physical interactions, smooth motion continuity, controlled cinematic camera movement, high visual quality, and accurate execution of the requested action.

FINAL INSTRUCTION:
Generate a visually consistent, realistic, smooth, and professionally cinematic video. Follow the user's description precisely. Preserve the original character and scene identity. Animate only what is necessary to accomplish the requested action. Never sacrifice visual consistency or natural movement for unnecessary cinematic effects.
Additional rules for 3D cartoon animation
ADDITIONAL RULES FOR 3D CARTOON ANIMATION

Maintain consistent 3D character models, including facial design, colors, textures, proportions, and clothing.
Preserve the original cartoon art style throughout the entire video.
Use smooth, professional 3D character animation with expressive and believable movements.
Maintain consistent character proportions, even during exaggerated cartoon actions.
Use natural cartoon facial expressions, eye movements, blinking, and lip synchronization when speaking.
Maintain consistent 3D geometry, textures, materials, and object shapes.
Use believable cartoon physics while preserving the intended animation style.
Ensure natural contact between characters and objects.
Maintain consistent lighting, shadows, reflections, and environmental details.
Avoid character model deformation, unwanted morphing, texture flickering, and inconsistent proportions.
Use expressive body language and appealing character poses.
Maintain smooth transitions between animation poses and actions.
Preserve the original 3D cartoon visual style. Do not unexpectedly switch to photorealism, 2D animation, anime, or another art style.
Maintain consistent camera perspective, depth, and cinematic composition.
Keep the animation visually polished, clean, colorful, and professionally rendered.

FINAL 3D CARTOON INSTRUCTION:
Prioritize consistent character design, appealing animation, expressive emotions, smooth movement, believable cartoon physics, and consistent 3D rendering quality. Preserve the original visual style throughout the entire video.
